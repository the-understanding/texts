## Preface

&emsp;Here, I would like to summarize the overall picture of basic concepts from the perspective of information manipulation, regarding the naive thinking procedures consistently used to understand things, ranging from familiar daily events to complex problems. For conciseness, this summary adopts the following format:

- Divided the whole into 10 chapters, each prefixed with a simple question as an introduction to its respective theme.
- Emphasized grasping the overall perspective gained from the structure rather than the accuracy of individual explanations. Each item is limited to presenting the concept in its heading, delegating detailed explanations and concrete examples elsewhere, while listing related terms based on similarity of meaning, inclusion, antonymy, co-occurrence, etc.
- To avoid discussions regarding the interpretation of cited sources, no citations based on literature or books were made. Instead, general concepts such as those listed on Wikipedia were arranged with links as much as possible. However, some terms not listed on Wikipedia or that are uncommon have also been included at the author's discretion.
- Historical debates and backgrounds of each item are omitted.
- Items dealing with concrete objects are outside the scope of this summary, though examples are provided for reference in some items.
- The following types of items were intentionally omitted or kept as brief as possible:
	- Most of philosophy, including metaphysics, epistemology, semiotics, philosophy of language, etc.
	- Politics, economics, finance, business, law, accounting, etc.
	- Psychology, sociology, cultural anthropology, pedagogy, history, etc.
	- Education, sports, embodiment, etc.
	- Art, literature, narrative, culture, etc.
	- Religion, thought, ideology, policy, ethics, dignity, human rights, justice, etc.
	- Mind, personality, view of life, purpose in life, worries, etc.
	- Emotions (empathy, trust, hope, romance, affection, etc.), enjoyment, preferences, etc.
	- Values, aesthetic sense, taste, humor, personal impressions, intuition, non-reproducible ideas, etc.
	- Communication, interpersonal relationships, services, etc.
	- Injustice, corruption, prejudice, discrimination, etc.

&emsp;This summary is intended to extend the processing methods for information received daily in various situations from their underlying principles to daily-level thinking, connecting them to the methodologies of various academic disciplines in the process. It is created as a fundamental set of concepts to organize information for a given task and improve foresight for subsequent operations. While it has the potential to be useful to anyone in a practical sense, it may also contain personal views of the author that are not conventional. Therefore, users are requested to use it at their own risk upon publication and are encouraged to modify and adapt it as appropriate. Differences in information granularity between items remain a challenge, and there are many instances where similar concepts across various fields have been gathered, resulting in a sequence or categorization that differs from standard textbooks. However, the author intends to continue reviewing and revising it.
